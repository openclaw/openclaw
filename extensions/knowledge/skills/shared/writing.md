# Writing pages

Write like a good human notetaker: short, specific, names first.

- **Lead with who and what.** "Ayaan: start the Vercel token pass." Not "The
  Ayaan-addressed update planned to implement…".
- **One qualifier, only when it matters.** If a deployment status is genuinely
  contradictory, say so once ("deployment status unclear"). Don't attach "this is
  not proof of…", "not independently verified" or "no deadline was stated" to
  ordinary items. Absent facts are simply absent.
- **Let the verb carry certainty:** proposed, planned, agreed, reported, decided.
  Attribute speculation as speculation, and never state an outcome or completion
  the source doesn't show.
- **Keep the specifics:** numbers, dates, amounts, names and who decided what,
  exactly as the source states them.
- **No notes to yourself.** Never write instructions, reminders or reasoning about
  your process into a page ("keep this chronology", "do not assign by guess",
  "withheld from profiles: …"). Withheld material and attribution reasoning live
  in your work log, structured evidence and `attribution_confidence`.
- **No filler.** Don't write "X was discussed in the cited update." Link entities
  inline where they matter in the prose.
- **No personal contact details** (email addresses, phone numbers) in page prose.
- **Citations:** one readable citation per claim, at the end of the sentence or
  bullet. Merge adjacent ranges (`lines 93–98`, not `lines 93–93` plus
  `lines 94–98`). Never cite a whole transcript.
- **Quotes** must be verbatim and worth reading: a decision, a position or a
  memorable framing. A garbled or trivial line is not a notable quote; use the
  "none" placeholder instead.

Quick self-check before publishing a page: would a teammate who missed the
meeting read this and come away informed, without wading through caveats?
